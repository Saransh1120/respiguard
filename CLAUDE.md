# RespiGuard

Chest-worn asthma monitor built around an ESP32-S3. It learns the wearer's own
baseline for oxygen saturation, breathing rate and heart rate, then flags an
episode when those drift away from that personal baseline while a wheeze
classifier is also firing. Movement suppresses the alert, so exertion is not
mistaken for an attack.

Owner: Saransh. Built for a hackathon, hand-soldered from breakout modules.

## Decisions already made — do not reopen these

- **AMS1117-3.3 is rejected.** Its 1 V dropout cannot hold 3.3 V from a Li-Po as
  the cell falls toward 3.0 V. A buck-boost module replaces it.
- **Li-Po is 1000 mAh, not 300 mAh.** 300 mAh gives roughly two hours.
- **TP4056 must be the three-chip version** (TP4056 + DW01 + FS8205). The
  single-chip board has no over-discharge protection and will kill the cell.
- **The OLED is monochrome.** Renders showing a colour screen are wrong;
  SSD1306 cannot do it.
- **USB-C sits on the end wall, not the front face.** The DevKitC-1 is 63 mm
  long and its USB is on a short end, which cannot face the 54 mm depth.
- **The microphone faces the chest, on the base.** Breath sounds travel better
  through the body than through air, so a top-edge mic is worse for wheeze.
- **Enclosure is 80 × 54 × 26 mm.** A 45 mm square watch-style case cannot hold
  a DevKit plus five breakouts plus a battery. That form factor needs a custom
  fabricated PCB, which is a separate project.

## Firmware architecture

`wokwi_sketch.txt` holds the real logic and is already proven in simulation:

- Baseline learning over a window (20 s in simulation, 3 days on hardware)
- Deviation scoring against that personal baseline, not absolute thresholds:
  wheeze +2, SpO₂ drop over 3 points +2, RR rise over 20 % +1, HR rise over
  15 % +1
- Exertion veto: −2 when movement exceeds 0.35 and no wheeze is present
- States OK / WATCH / ALERT at scores 0 / 2 / 4, with a 4 s hold so the display
  does not flicker
- Outputs: OLED, green and red LEDs, haptic double-pulse, short-press event
  marking and 1.5 s hold to relearn

Porting to hardware means rewriting `sampleSensors()` only. Everything else
carries over unchanged.

## Pin map (ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 defaults, confirm against actual wiring)

```
I2C          SDA 8, SCL 9
I2S mic      BCLK 4, WS 5, SD 6
LEDs         green 15, red 16
Motor        17 (through 1 kΩ into 2N2222 base)
Button       18 (INPUT_PULLUP, other side to GND)
Battery ADC  1 (junction of two 100 kΩ resistors)
```

Expected I2C addresses: OLED 0x3C, MAX30102 0x57, BME680 0x76/0x77,
BMI270 0x68/0x69. No conflicts.

Chip IDs the self-test checks: BME680 0xD0 → 0x61, MAX30102 0xFF → 0x15,
BMI270 0x00 → 0x24. A BMP280 sold as a BME680 reads 0x58.

## Machine learning track

The wheeze classifier is the long pole and needs no hardware, so it should run
in parallel from the start.

- Training data: ICBHI 2017 Respiratory Sound Database — 920 recordings, 126
  patients, labelled for wheeze and crackle. Free on Kaggle, and also on
  Harvard Dataverse as a single 1.84 GB zip with no login required
  (doi:10.7910/DVN/HT6PKI, file id 7127117).
- Known problem: ICBHI was recorded with a chest-contact stethoscope while the
  INMP441 sits under clothing. This domain gap means an ICBHI-only model will
  underperform on real hardware.
- Mitigation: pretrain on ICBHI, record 50–100 normal-breathing samples on the
  actual hardware (no ethics issue, it is the negative class), then fine-tune.
- Real patient validation is a later step through a clinician with written
  consent, and should be described honestly as future work, never as done.
